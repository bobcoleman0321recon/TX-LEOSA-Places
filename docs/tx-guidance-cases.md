## What a Texas carry or force case looks like, start to finish

| Step | Texas rule | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warrantless arrest | Any person, peace officer or not, may arrest without a warrant for a felony or an offense against the public peace committed in their presence or view (art. 14.01(a)); a peace officer for any offense in view ((b)). Officers may also arrest in listed circumstances, including persons found in suspicious places reasonably showing a felony or a ch. 42 offense (art. 14.03(a)(1)) | CCP arts. 14.01, 14.03 | Verified Nov 8 |
| Disarming | A peace officer acting lawfully may disarm a person whenever the officer reasonably believes it necessary for anyone's protection, and must return the handgun before releasing the person from the scene if the person is not a threat and is not arrested (art. 14.03(h)) | CCP art. 14.03(h) | Verified Nov 8 |
| Citation instead of arrest | For a Class C misdemeanor (such as weapons-only trespass under § 30.05(d-3)), an officer may issue a citation instead of taking the person to a magistrate (art. 14.06(b)). Class A and B citations are limited to a short list that does not include weapons offenses ((c), (d)). § 46.03 felonies mean arrest | CCP art. 14.06 | Verified Nov 8 |
| The magistrate | An arrested person goes before a magistrate without unnecessary delay, within 48 hours, and is told the charge, the right to counsel, the right to remain silent, the right to have a lawyer present during any interview and to end it, and that any statement may be used against them (art. 15.17(a)) | CCP art. 15.17 | Verified Nov 8 |
| Statements | A written or recorded statement from custodial interrogation is admissible only after the warnings and a knowing waiver (art. 38.22 §§ 2, 3). **But** § 5 admits statements that are the res gestae of the arrest or offense, statements that do not stem from custodial interrogation, and voluntary statements. What a person says at the scene before custody can be used | CCP art. 38.22 | Verified Nov 8 |
| Bail | Bail is set by the rules in art. 17.15 (nature of the offense, including an "offense involving violence"; ability to pay; safety of the victim, law enforcement and the community; criminal history). A person charged with an offense involving violence, which includes murder (§ 19.02) and aggravated assault (§ 22.02), may not be released on personal bond (art. 17.03(b-2)). Texas Constitution: all prisoners are bailable except in capital cases when the proof is evident (art. I, § 11) | CCP arts. 17.03, 17.15; Tex. Const. art. I, §§ 10, 11 | Verified Nov 8 |
| Grand jury | No felony prosecution without a grand jury indictment (Tex. Const. art. I, § 10), unless waived. In practice a county district attorney presents a shooting to a grand jury, which can return a "no bill." Example: Jefferson County's grand jury attorney said every possible charge is presented and the grand jury is told the justification and self-defense rules before voting (PA News, Jan. 31, 2023) | Tex. Const. art. I, § 10; news report | Verified (Constitution); news is that source's account |
| Who investigates | No special unit covers a private person's or a retiree's shooting. The local police agency investigates and sends the case to the county district attorney. State code sets no formula for when the Texas Rangers take a case; they usually act on a request from a local agency, a prosecutor or the governor (KUT / Houston Public Media, July 15, 2026). This project found no rule sending a retiree's shooting to the Rangers | News report (practice) | Partly verified: no statute found; practice from news |
| The jury charge | Justification is a defense; once evidence raises it, the jury is told that a reasonable doubt on it requires acquittal (§§ 2.03, 9.02). A defendant gets a self-defense instruction if any evidence raises it, strong or weak (*Gamino*). The State Bar's Texas Criminal Pattern Jury Charges (Criminal Defenses volume; deadly force is chapter 32 in the 2018 edition) are sold, not published free; not read | Penal Code §§ 2.03, 9.02; *Gamino*; State Bar catalog | Statute and case verified; pattern charge not read |
| Training as evidence | In *Guyger*, an off-duty officer's own training (take cover, call for help rather than enter) and a fellow officer's testimony supported the jury's finding that her belief in the need for deadly force was not reasonable. Texas's test is the reasonable person (§ 1.07 "reasonable belief," not yet read at the source). This project reads training as cutting both ways (counsel question 5) | *Guyger*; Penal Code § 9.32 | Case read; § 1.07 not read |
| "Criminal activity" | Being engaged in criminal activity removes only the presumption of reasonableness (and the no-retreat protection), not the right of self-defense. In *Cuevas* the court reversed a murder conviction because the State told jurors that a person committing a crime could not claim self-defense at all. The prosecutor there counted unlawful firearm possession among the crimes. Whether carrying into a § 46.03 place without the certificate is "criminal activity" for a LEOSA retiree remains counsel question 2 | *Cuevas* (2026); Penal Code §§ 9.31(e), 9.32(b)–(d) | Verified Nov 8 (opinion read) |

## Penalty grading that matters to a retiree

| Conduct | Grade | Cite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrying in most § 46.03 places without an exemption | Third-degree felony; Class A at sporting events, hospitals, amusement parks, civil commitment facilities | § 46.03 (Layer 5) |
| Weapons-only trespass after notice | Class C ($200); Class A if told in person and you stay | § 30.05(d-3) |
| Unholstered display; carrying while intoxicated | Class A | § 46.02(a-5), (a-6), (b) |
| Threatening with a gun, not justified | Aggravated assault, second-degree felony; no personal bond | §§ 22.02; CCP art. 17.03(b-2) |
| Pointing a gun | Deadly conduct, Class A; firing at people or an occupied building or vehicle, third-degree felony | § 22.05 |

## Real cases (for the app's Real cases tab)

Court opinions are named by caption. People in news reports are described, not named.

| Case | Court or source | What happened | Lesson | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Cuevas v. State*, No. PD-0144-25 | Tex. Crim. App., Apr. 2, 2026 (published) | Murder conviction after a shooting during a drug sale; the prosecutor told jurors a person committing a crime could not claim self-defense. Reversed for a new trial | Criminal activity costs the presumption, not the defense; and what a prosecutor argues matters | Opinion read |
| *Gamino v. State*, 537 S.W.3d 507 | Tex. Crim. App. 2017 | A man drew a gun on three men who threatened him and his girlfriend outside Fort Worth bars; off-duty officers working security arrested him; convicted of aggravated assault; new trial because the jury was not given self-defense | Drawing to make a threat stop can be justified (§ 9.04), but it still started with an arrest and a conviction | Opinion read |
| *Braughton v. State*, 569 S.W.3d 592 | Tex. Crim. App. 2018 | A son came out armed when his father was in a fistfight with a neighbor and shot the neighbor, who was stepping away; no weapon was found on the neighbor. Murder, 20 years, upheld | Defense of a third person ends when the attack ends; a jury may disbelieve claims of a reach for a gun | Opinion read |
| *Guyger v. State*, No. 05-19-01236-CR | Tex. App.—Dallas, Aug. 5, 2021 (not designated for publication) | An off-duty Dallas officer entered the wrong apartment, believed the resident was an intruder and shot him. Murder, 10 years, affirmed. Her training (cover, call for help) was used to show her belief was unreasonable | Training cuts both ways; a mistaken belief must still be reasonable; being an officer is no shield | Opinion read |
| Retired Beaumont officer, Port Arthur | News report (PA News, Jan. 31, 2023) | A retired Beaumont officer fatally shot a man outside the man's home in November 2022; two guns were involved. About two months later a Jefferson County grand jury declined to indict "on the grounds of self-defense" | Even a no-bill takes a full investigation and a grand jury; the outcome was never automatic | News report (that source's account) |
| Pasadena, 2007 | News report (ABC News, June 30, 2008) | A homeowner called 911 to report two men breaking into a neighbor's house, was told repeatedly by the dispatcher not to go out, and shot both men. A Harris County grand jury declined to indict after two weeks of testimony; the DA said deadly force "is carefully limited in Texas law" | The property rules exist, but the 911 recording became evidence, and the result took seven months and a grand jury | News report |
| *Salinas v. Texas*, 570 U.S. 178 | U.S. Supreme Court 2013 (plurality) | A Houston murder suspect answered police questions voluntarily but went silent when asked about the shotgun; his silence was used at trial | Say out loud that you are invoking your rights | Opinion summary verified (NY build) |
