New sources read Nov 8: Government Code §§ 411.171, 411.173, 411.199, 411.205; S.B. 706 (89th Leg., 2025); DPS Reciprocity FAQs. Every cite is in `sources.md`. Draft: not reviewed by an attorney.

The answer: Texas has **two certificate paths**, and both end at the same exemption (Penal Code § 46.15(a)(5)), which lifts Texas's carry and places laws. The **service path** (Occupations Code § 1701.357(b), (c)): any Texas state or local agency may certify a retired Texas commissioned peace officer, wherever they now live. The **residence path** ((b-1)): TCOLE certifies any § 926C retiree through a qualified handgun instructor, but only Texas residents (TCOLE's form). A third, discretionary route lets a Texas state agency certify other retirees ((h)). So A, B, C and likely E can get it; most visitors (D) cannot and rely on LEOSA alone at § 46.03 places, except a retired Texas officer living elsewhere, or a visitor a Texas state agency agrees to certify. Corrected Nov 8 after reading UT System Police Policy 410 and re-reading § 1701.357; the first draft said residence alone decided it. Two things soften the gap: since 2021 anyone 21 or older who is not barred may carry a handgun in Texas without a license (§ 46.02), and since Sept. 1, 2025 Texas recognizes every other state's carry license (§ 411.173(b), S.B. 706). Hardware is the same for everyone: Texas has no magazine or hollow-point limits.

Unlike New York, losing LEOSA status in Texas does not end the right to carry. It ends the place exemptions.

## What the app should say (for `rules_tx.js`)

- Profile picker: "Do you live in Texas?" decides whether the certificate path is shown. A visitor sees: "TCOLE gives its certificate only to retirees who live in Texas. If you retired from a Texas agency, a Texas agency may still certify you. Otherwise in Texas you carry under LEOSA, and Texas's places law may still apply to you; see each place's status."
- License card: "If you hold a Texas license to carry, Texas law requires you to show it with your driver's license when an officer asks for ID while you are carrying (Gov't Code § 411.205). If you hold another state's license, show it too."
- Lapse card: "If your LEOSA qualification lapses you may still carry a handgun in Texas if you are 21 or older and not barred (Penal Code § 46.02), but every place in § 46.03 applies to you."

## Open questions (raised Nov 8, 2026)

9. Is a visitor carrying under a recognized home-state license (§ 411.173(b)) "licensed to carry a handgun under Subchapter H" for Penal Code purposes (the § 46.03 definition of "license holder"; §§ 30.06, 30.07; the college exception in § 46.03(a)(1)(B))? § 411.173 sits in Subchapter H, which suggests yes. DPS's reciprocity FAQ says only that visitors must follow Texas law. No AG opinion found yet.
10. Profile E: does TCOLE issue the certificate to a retired military police officer living in Texas, and is a service branch a "federal ... law enforcement agency" for the § 46.15(a)(5) photo ID? Is a military retiree a "retired officer of the United States" under § 411.199(g)? **Read Nov 8: DoD Instruction 5525.12** (Layer 1 in `../../STATE_WORKFLOW.md`). The DoD card is issued by the DoD Component the officer separated from, and its glossary calls those units "DoD law enforcement agencies," which supports reading the issuer as a federal law enforcement agency. The card reads "DoD Law Enforcement Officer (Separated)" and "separated in good standing," not "retired." TCOLE's form asks federal applicants for an agency ID stating "retired or separated" status, so the certificate looks open to profile E. The harder question is § 46.15(a)(5), which asks for ID that verifies the officer is an "honorably retired peace officer or other qualified retired law enforcement officer": a card saying "Separated" plus the § 926C wording arguably verifies the second, but no Texas source says so (same issue as `tx-definitions.md` question 3). DoD does not provide qualification to separated officers, so a profile E retiree in Texas qualifies through TCOLE or a certified instructor in any case. **Service programs read Nov 8** (Layer 1): the ID must come from the retiree's own service. The wording varies: DoDI 5525.12's model says "Separated"; the Marine Corps card (MCO 5580.4A, 2025) says "Retired or Separated"; the Navy's card wording is not published on the pages read. A Marine Corps card expires after five years, so a Texas certificate holder also needs the card to be current.
11. What does a Texas officer see on a records check: is an LTC (Texas or recognized) or a TCOLE certificate visible in TLETS? Lead: DPS law enforcement personnel page.
