# TX — Layer 5: sensitive places crosswalk (draft)

As of Nov 7, 2026. **Draft statuses; the `retired` column follows Bob's decision of Nov 7, 2026 (below).** Groups are defined in `tx-definitions.md`.

**Decision, Nov 7, 2026 (open question 1):** for a LEOSA retiree without the Texas certificate (`retired`, which includes most visiting retirees), § 46.03 bans on **privately owned places** are **C — Restricted for most carriers, check your status**. Reason: § 926C(b) preserves only owner bans and bans on state or local government property; a state ban on a privately owned bar, racetrack, amusement park, hospital or stadium is neither, so LEOSA arguably overrides it, but no Texas court or AG opinion has said so. This project reads it that way and lists a counsel question; it does not tell the user the place is clear. § 46.03 places that are **government property** stay **R** for `retired` under § 926C(b)(2). Places where **federal law** applies on its own (§ 922(q) school grounds, airport secured areas) stay **R**. An owner's own notice (§ 30.05) is always honored (§ 926C(b)(1)). Same approach as New Jersey for questions the courts have not settled. R = Restricted (cited); C = Restricted for most carriers — check your status (cited); N = No known restriction mapped — not a clearance. Mapped categories are never N.

Penal Code § 46.03 "premises" means a building or part of one; parking areas, driveways and walkways are not premises (§ 46.03(c)(4)).
